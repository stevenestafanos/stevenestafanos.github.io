Design Studio, Fall 2024

Medical Clinic

245 Dal

A neighbourhood medical clinic on Dalhousie Street in Ottawa's ByWard Market, planned to lease as little space as possible, then reworked to share its entry with a sports therapy centre on a new second floor.

Brief
Medical clinic for two doctors at 245 Dalhousie Street, Ottawa, then a second floor for a separate business

The brief

Two doctors want a new office in the ByWard Market, serving appointments and walk-in patients from the neighbourhood. Rent is expensive, so they want to lease the least space that still gives a well-run office, with a relaxed atmosphere that eases the anxiety of a medical visit.

The receptionist controls the movement of patients. Exam rooms and consulting offices need acoustic privacy, the doctors want to come and go without crossing the waiting room, and every area has to be barrier free.

Sorting the rooms

The bubble parti separates patient access from staff access, marks the meeting points where the two cross (reception, the nurse's station, the exam and consulting rooms) and places sound barriers around the rooms that need privacy. The adjacency matrix records which rooms have to sit next to each other and which should not.

First scheme: the clinic in two and a half bays

The first plan fits the whole clinic into two and a half structural bays of the base building, at an efficiency of 88.6 per cent. Patients enter through a vestibule into the waiting room. Reception and the nurse's station sit between the waiting room and the exam and consulting rooms, and staff use the rear door through the staff room. The exam and consulting room walls are sound insulated.

Adding a second floor

The developer then added a second floor for a separate business, chosen from the uses the zoning permits. A sports rehabilitation centre was chosen, and its programme was worked out in consultation with a registered massage therapist, as the clinic he would want to practise in: treatment rooms, a chiropractic room, a gym, and a wet treatment room with two tubs for contrast baths and a sauna. A hand-wash station sits at the end of the treatment corridor, beside the treatment rooms, so therapists can wash between clients without going through the bathrooms or back past reception. The two businesses share one entry: the vestibule now holds the stair and elevator to the upper floor as well as the clinic door, so the plan grew by a half bay to three full bays and the ground floor was replanned around the new entry.

Zoning sets the site plan: no setback at the street, 3 m at each side and 7.5 m at the rear, where the lots behind are zoned residential. Because the site is not square, the building is held 3.5 m off the left side so the full rear setback holds without cutting its depth. The site plan allows parking for 16 cars behind the building, plus a dedicated bay for waste collection. The first three bays hold the clinic and the sports centre; more bays can be leased to other businesses.

The resolved design

Net usable area is 67 per cent of gross on the first floor and 66.5 per cent on the second, down from 88.6 per cent in the first scheme. The drop comes from the shared entry with its stair and elevator, the mechanical shaft in the middle of the plan, and the larger number of bathrooms the sports centre needed. The final calculation also counts less as usable: bathrooms, janitor's closets, the entry, halls and shafts are all left out of the net area, where the first scheme left out only the corridors.

On the first floor, the nurse's station has no window of its own; it borrows daylight from the waiting room and its curtain wall. Upstairs, the gym is separated from the reception and waiting room by a glazed wall, so it stays in view and the floor feels open. The chiropractic room has a removable partition, so it can be opened to the gym or closed for privacy, and treatment room 1 has two doors for the same reason. Treatment rooms 2 and 3 are set up mainly for massage therapy and physiotherapy. The locker room is mainly for storing personal effects and opens to the wet treatment room; patients change in the three large bathrooms, each with a shower, which serve as washroom, shower and change room in one. With two or three patients changing at a time, one person using a bathroom for something else does not hold anyone up.
