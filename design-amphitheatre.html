Cultural Centre

Concept The Amphitheatre

A brick crescent wraps a stepped amphitheatre around a water feature that serves as splash pad, stage and rink.

The brief

One of eight Ottawa sites offered for a neighbourhood cultural centre, a City facility run with the local community association. Every site shared a core programme: vestibule, lobby, snack bar, first aid, a multipurpose room of 220 to 240 m², kitchen, offices, washrooms and services.

At 16 Rowley the museum’s own programme replaced the optional activities: 520 m² of exhibition space, at least 180 m² of artifact storage and 50 m² of administration. That put the net area at roughly 1,290 to 1,500 m².

The site

16 Rowley Avenue is the Nepean Museum, in a former library building in City View, in Ottawa’s west end beside Algonquin College. The museum moved into the building in the late 1980s. The lot, about 7,660 m², also holds a ball diamond, a basketball court and two parking lots. The houses around it are mostly mid-century, with infill replacing older ones.

The idea: a hemicycle for the neighbourhood

The project looks to the Roman amphitheatre, and to the hemicycle as a plan for gathering. A water feature takes the place of the arena, tiered seating curves around it, and a crescent of building sits beneath and behind the tiers.

Amphitheatres and buildings shaped like them have long served democracy, learning and entertainment. The centre celebrates the same things: a place to gather and meet, to work together, to learn and to be entertained. City View is a residential neighbourhood with few third places, somewhere other than home or work, and the centre is meant to be one.

The water feature

The water feature is the arena: a children’s splash pad whose jets can also run as a fountain, flooded as an ice rink in winter, and covered to make a stage. The goal was a centre that works in all four seasons: few people will sit outdoors through an Ottawa winter, so the bowl becomes a rink instead of standing empty. A long curved brick wall closes the far side of the water feature.

The building beneath the seating

Under the tiers, the building takes on the museum’s role and adds a small conference centre: a restaurant and kitchen, two rentable halls of 150 m², two rentable classrooms of 50 m², and the museum’s galleries, storage and administration. Glazed stair towers at each end carry visitors up through the building.

From matrix to hemicycle

Work began with an adjacency matrix of twenty spaces and a bubble diagram that puts the entry at the centre, with food on one side and the museum on the other. Section sketches stack the lobby, eatery, museum and washrooms beneath a raked stair. A plan sketch labelled “Nepean Hemicycle” fixes the curved form, and the first Revit site plan was drawn over a hand sketch.
